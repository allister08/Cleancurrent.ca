Questions and answers

The things people usually ask before booking. If yours isn't here, just ask.

Getting started

How much does it cost?

We quote each job up front. The price depends on travel, time on site, and what you need delivered: raw footage, an edited clip, or a written summary. Tell us about the job and we'll send you a quote.

What do you need from us?

What needs looking at, where it is, roughly how deep, and when you need it done. Photos or a drawing help. For a boat, its length and where it's berthed.

How soon can you come?

It depends on the weather and what else is booked. Tell us your deadline when you get in touch and we'll tell you honestly whether we can make it.

On the day

Do you put divers in the water?

No. The ROV goes down on a tether and we fly it from the surface, so nobody gets in the water.

Does my boat need to come out?

No. We film it where it sits, at the wharf, on a mooring or at anchor.

Can I watch?

Yes. The live camera feed comes up to a laptop on the wharf or the boat, and you can ask us to look closer at anything.

What if the water is murky or the weather is bad?

Visibility and weather change day to day. We check conditions with you before the dive, and if the camera won't be able to see what you need, we'll talk about moving the day.

Do you need permits?

If the job needs a permit or a port notification, we'll work out what's required with you before the dive.

Afterwards

What do I get?

The full recorded video, an edited clip of the parts that matter, still images, and a short written summary. See a sample report.

Will you tell me what needs fixing?

We describe what the footage shows and point out anything that stands out. Whether something needs repair is up to your surveyor, engineer or mooring service.

Is the footage colour corrected?

Not unless you ask. What you see on this site is straight off the camera.

About us

Where do you work?

We are based in Halifax and Dartmouth and work across Atlantic Canada, and further afield for larger jobs.

How deep can the ROV go?

It is rated to 150 m (500 ft) and runs on a 300 m (1,000 ft) tether.

Got something that needs a look?

Tell us what it is and where. One of us usually replies the same day.
